# ADR-0000: Title (short, decision-phrased — "X over Y for Z")

- **Status:** Proposed | Accepted | Superseded by ADR-XXXX
- **Date:** YYYY-MM-DD
- **Related:** ADR-XXXX, ADR-YYYY

ADRs are not documentation. They are permanent engineering decisions. Write
for an engineer reading this two years from now who must decide whether the
decision still holds.

## Context

The situation as it stood: constraints, forces, and facts. No solutions here.

## Problem

The specific question that had to be answered. One or two sentences,
phrased as a question or a forced choice.

## Decision

"We will …" — active voice, unambiguous. Include the key mechanics only if
they are part of the commitment itself.

## Alternatives considered

Each alternative with the honest reason it lost — "rejected because X",
never a strawman. If an alternative becomes right later, say what would
make it right (and repeat it under Future review criteria).

## Trade-offs

What we knowingly gave up by deciding this way. If this section is empty,
the ADR is describing a fact, not a decision — delete it or dig deeper.

## Consequences

What becomes true because of this decision — good and bad, structural and
procedural. What does this decision force or forbid elsewhere?

## Future review criteria

The evidence that would justify reopening this decision. Specific and
observable ("sustained queue depth > X", "vendor archived the project"),
never "if needed".
